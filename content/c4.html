Key ideas

Real oscillators lose energy to friction or resistance. With a damping force −bv-bv−bv the amplitude decays exponentially. A spring–mass system with friction and an RLC circuit obey the same differential equation, with m↔Lm\leftrightarrow Lm↔L, b↔Rb\leftrightarrow Rb↔R, k↔1/Ck\leftrightarrow 1/Ck↔1/C and x↔qx\leftrightarrow qx↔q.

Setting up the equation for a mass in water

Hang a block from a spring and let it oscillate inside a beaker of water. Its weight and the buoyant force only move the rest position a little, so we measure the displacement xxx from that new rest position. When the block is at xxx and moving with velocity v=dxdtv=\dfrac{dx}{dt}v=dtdx​, two forces act on it.

  1. Spring force. Fs=−kxF_s=-kxFs​=−kx. It always pulls the block back toward x=0x=0x=0.
  2. Damping force. At low speed the water pushes against the motion with a force proportional to the speed, Fd=−bv=−bdxdtF_d=-bv=-b\dfrac{dx}{dt}Fd​=−bv=−bdtdx​. The constant bbb is the damping coefficient, measured in kg/s. A thicker liquid such as oil or honey has a larger bbb.
  3. Newton's second law. md2xdt2=−kx−bdxdtm\dfrac{d^2x}{dt^2}=-kx-b\dfrac{dx}{dt}mdt2d2x​=−kx−bdtdx​, which gives md2xdt2+bdxdt+kx=0m\dfrac{d^2x}{dt^2}+b\dfrac{dx}{dt}+kx=0mdt2d2x​+bdtdx​+kx=0
  4. Divide by mmm. d2xdt2+2γdxdt+ω02x=0,γ=b2m,ω0=km\dfrac{d^2x}{dt^2}+2\gamma\dfrac{dx}{dt}+\omega_0^2x=0,\qquad \gamma=\frac b{2m},\quad \omega_0=\sqrt{\frac km}dt2d2x​+2γdtdx​+ω02​x=0,γ=2mb​,ω0​=mk​​ Here γ\gammaγ tells how strongly the liquid damps the motion and ω0\omega_0ω0​ is the angular frequency the block would have with no damping.

The LC circuit is a spring and a pendulum in disguise

Compare the three equations. The LC circuit obeys Ld2qdt2+qC=0L\dfrac{d^2q}{dt^2}+\dfrac{q}{C}=0Ldt2d2q​+Cq​=0, a spring obeys md2xdt2+kx=0m\dfrac{d^2x}{dt^2}+kx=0mdt2d2x​+kx=0 and a pendulum at small angles obeys mℓd2θdt2+mgθ=0m\ell\dfrac{d^2\theta}{dt^2}+mg\theta=0mℓdt2d2θ​+mgθ=0. They have exactly the same form, so they behave in exactly the same way. Charge on the capacitor behaves like the stretch of the spring or the angle of the pendulum, and current behaves like velocity. The inductor fights any change in current just as mass fights any change in velocity, so LLL acts like mmm. A small capacitor is a stiff spring, so 1/C1/C1/C acts like kkk.

AC through a resistor, an inductor and a capacitor (LRC circuits)

How is AC voltage generated? 
Ac is generated through Faraday's law, which states that a changing magnetic flux induces an EMF. So, in a generator we can rotate a coil of conducting write in a magnetic field and generate time dependent voltage. Simulation below illustrates this phenomena: 


An AC generator converts mechanical energy into electrical energy through electromagnetic induction. When a coil rotates inside a magnetic field, the changing magnetic flux induces an alternating electromotive force (EMF).

Why do we even need AC voltages? 

We primarily need AC voltage to transmit signals over large distances. Imagine a power station generating electricity 200200200 km away from your home. We need to: 
Generate electrical energy ⟹\Longrightarrow⟹  Transport over long distances ⟹\Longrightarrow⟹ Deliver to home safely with the voltage the appliances require. Now, we know that, P=VI⇒I=PVP=VI\Rightarrow I=\frac{P}{V}P=VI⇒I=VP​ So larger the voltage higher the current and since Ploss=I2RP_{loss} = I^2RPloss​=I2R the loss is large as well (wasted as heat). Therefore, we need some device that will raise/lower the voltage accordingly. This device is known as a transformer, which can either increase (step up transformer) or decrease voltage (step down transformer) when needed. However, transformers need AC voltage because of electromagnetic induction (Again Faraday's law, ϵ=−NdϕBdt\epsilon = -N\frac{d\phi_B}{dt}ϵ=−NdtdϕB​​).  

Consequently, an AC source gives a voltage that changes like a sine wave, v=V0sin⁡ωtv=V_0\sin\omega tv=V0​sinωt with ω=2πf\omega=2\pi fω=2πf. Below we find how the current in each part follows this voltage. The results explain every graph in the simulations that follow.

1. Resistor

Ohm's law holds at every instant, vR=iRv_R=iRvR​=iR. With vR=V0sin⁡ωtv_R=V_0\sin\omega tvR​=V0​sinωt,

i=V0Rsin⁡ωt=I0sin⁡ωt,I0=V0Ri=\frac{V_0}{R}\sin\omega t=I_0\sin\omega t,\qquad I_0=\frac{V_0}{R}i=RV0​​sinωt=I0​sinωt,I0​=RV0​​

The current rises and falls exactly with the voltage, so they are in phase (ϕ=0\phi=0ϕ=0). R does not depend on frequency, and the power p=i2Rp=i^2Rp=i2R is never negative, so the resistor always turns energy into heat.

2. Inductor, starting from the magnetic field and Lenz's law

  1. Field of the coil. A long coil with NNN turns, length lll and cross-section AAA carrying current iii has a field B=μ0Nl iB=\mu_0\dfrac{N}{l}\,iB=μ0​lN​i inside.
  2. Flux. The flux through one turn is ΦB=BA=μ0NAl i\Phi_B=BA=\dfrac{\mu_0NA}{l}\,iΦB​=BA=lμ0​NA​i. All NNN turns are linked, so the total flux linkage is NΦB=μ0N2Al i=Li,L=μ0N2AlN\Phi_B=\frac{\mu_0N^2A}{l}\,i=Li,\qquad L=\frac{\mu_0N^2A}{l}NΦB​=lμ0​N2A​i=Li,L=lμ0​N2A​ The self inductance LLL depends only on the shape of the coil.
  3. Faraday's law with Lenz's rule. When the current changes, the flux changes and an EMF is induced in the coil itself, ε=−d(NΦB)dt=−Ldidt\varepsilon=-\frac{d(N\Phi_B)}{dt}=-L\dfrac{di}{dt}ε=−dtd(NΦB​)​=−Ldtdi​ The minus sign is Lenz's law. The induced EMF always opposes the change that causes it, so it pushes back when iii rises and pushes forward when iii falls. This is the back EMF.
  4. Voltage across the inductor. To keep the current changing, the source must supply a voltage equal and opposite to the back EMF, vL=Ldidtv_L=L\dfrac{di}{dt}vL​=Ldtdi​
  5. With an AC current. Put i=I0sin⁡ωti=I_0\sin\omega ti=I0​sinωt, vL=Lddt(I0sin⁡ωt)=ωLI0cos⁡ωt=ωLI0sin⁡(ωt+90∘)v_L=L\frac{d}{dt}\left(I_0\sin\omega t\right)=\omega LI_0\cos\omega t=\omega LI_0\sin\left(\omega t+90^\circ\right)vL​=Ldtd​(I0​sinωt)=ωLI0​cosωt=ωLI0​sin(ωt+90∘) So the voltage reaches its peak a quarter cycle before the current. The current lags the voltage by 90∘90^\circ90∘.
  6. Inductive reactance. We can define reactance in a similar way to resistance in Ohm’s law. By comparing the voltage-current relationship (V=IR) with the voltage-current relationship for an inductor, we obtain an analogous quantity called inductive reactance, given by:XL=ωL=2πfLX_L=\omega L=2\pi fLXL​=ωL=2πfLThus, inductive reactance plays a role similar to resistance in an AC circuit and is also measured in ohms ((\Omega)). A higher frequency means the current must change faster, so the back EMF is bigger and less current flows.

Look at the graph. vLv_LvL​ is largest when iii crosses zero, because that is where iii changes fastest. vLv_LvL​ is zero when iii is at its peak, because there iii is momentarily not changing.

3. Capacitor

The charge on the plates follows the voltage, q=CvCq=Cv_Cq=CvC​. The current is the rate at which charge arrives, i=dqdt=CdvCdti=\dfrac{dq}{dt}=C\frac{dv_C}{dt}i=dtdq​=CdtdvC​​ With vC=V0sin⁡ωtv_C=V_0\sin\omega tvC​=V0​sinωt, i=ωCV0cos⁡ωt=I0sin⁡(ωt+90∘),I0=ωCV0=V0XC,XC=1ωCi=\omega CV_0\cos\omega t=I_0\sin\left(\omega t+90^\circ\right),\qquad I_0=\omega CV_0=\frac{V_0}{X_C},\quad X_C=\frac{1}{\omega C}i=ωCV0​cosωt=I0​sin(ωt+90∘),I0​=ωCV0​=XC​V0​​,XC​=ωC1​ The current reaches its peak a quarter cycle before the voltage, so it leads by 90∘90^\circ90∘. No charge crosses the gap. Electrons simply pile up on one plate and then the other. At high frequency the plates never have time to fill, so the capacitor passes current easily and XCX_CXC​ is small.
